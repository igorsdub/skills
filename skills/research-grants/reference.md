## What it does

`research-grants` guides the preparation, structuring, and review of competitive grant proposals for major funding agencies, including the National Science Foundation (NSF), National Institutes of Health (NIH), Department of Energy (DOE), Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA), and Taiwan National Science and Technology Council (NSTC).

The skill assists with each core phase of proposal development:
- **Opportunity Compliance:** Parses funding opportunity announcements (FOAs, RFAs, solicitations) to build requirement-to-section compliance matrices before drafting begins.
- **Specific Aims and Narratives:** Formulates persuasive, testable hypotheses and aim structures (independent rather than sequential/contingent aims).
- **Agency Review Criteria:** Aligns narrative framing with specific evaluation criteria, such as NSF Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts, NIH Significance, Innovation, and Approach, or DARPA Heilmeier Catechism.
- **Supporting Documents:** Guides authoring for budget justifications, facilities descriptions, biographical sketches, and data management plans.
- **Resubmissions:** Structures response-to-reviewers documents, addressing critique point-by-point without defensiveness.

## Common questions

**What is the NIH policy on AI assistance for grant applications?**

NIH policies explicitly state that applications substantially developed or written by AI are excluded. Applicants must preserve genuine ownership of substantive scientific ideas and original prose. `research-grants` acts as an advisor, compliance checker, and critique partner for investigator-authored text; it does not replace scientific leadership.

**Why should Specific Aims be independent rather than progressive?**

A common failure mode in grant proposals is creating sequential aims where Aim 2 cannot be pursued if Aim 1 yields negative results. Reviewers penalize this as high risk. The skill emphasizes designing independent, complementary aims that address the central hypothesis from different angles so that unexpected results in one aim do not derail the entire project.

**Does this skill generate official agency PDF forms?**

No. The skill provides Markdown templates, structural outlines, and text worksheets in `assets/`. Official forms and electronic submission packages (such as Research.gov, ASSIST, or Grants.gov workspace) must be completed in the respective government portals.

**How does the skill handle resubmissions?**

It structures an introduction section that acknowledges reviewers respectfully, outlines major revisions made to the text, summarizes new preliminary data acquired since the previous submission, and points directly to updated sections in the narrative.

## It's working if

- Every aim statement features a clear rationale, testable hypothesis, concise experimental plan, and expected outcomes.
- Aims are structurally independent, preventing single points of experimental failure.
- Broader impacts (for NSF) include concrete, measurable activities integrated with institutional resources rather than vague aspirations.
- Solicitations are cross-referenced for page limits, required attachments, and formatting constraints.
- No synthetic data, imaginary preliminary results, or fabricated citations are suggested.
